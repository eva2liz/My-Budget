Savings goals e.g. emergency fund, a car, a trip

Track money you're setting aside for something specific, separate from bills and debt. Use Add money to log a contribution directly on a goal, or link a goal to a category (e.g. "Savings") so every Check Register or CC Register transaction filed under that category counts toward it automatically -- its running total folds into "already saved" above, and every matching transaction is listed under that goal so you can see exactly what added up to it.

Recurring expenses e.g. rent, subscriptions

Recurring expenses are automatically logged next time you open the app. Monthly logs on the day you set, every month. Every 2 weeks and every 2 months log from any date you know it landed on (past or future) — future/past occurrences are calculated from there, same as biweekly income. Default paycheck applies to the specific paycheck you pick — since each new paycheck is its own entry, you may need to repick it once that paycheck has passed. Day-to-day, the Paychecks tab is the easiest place to assign bills each pay period.

Recurring income e.g. payday

Monthly recurring income logs automatically on the day you set. Every-2-weeks income (like a biweekly paycheck that lands on a different date each cycle, e.g. every other Friday) logs every 14 days starting from any date you know it landed on — pick a recent or upcoming payday, past or future ones are all calculated from it. Either way it shows on the Calendar tab in the color you pick, and previews in the Paycheck planner before it's even logged.

Active recurring income

Every recurring payday highlights the whole day in the color you picked for it. Recurring bills show as labeled tags in their category color on the day they're due, with a ✓ once that occurrence is marked paid. Click any payday or bill to open and edit it. One-time expenses and one-time income aren't shown here, since they don't repeat month to month — see the Check Register or the Paychecks tab for those.

Paycheck planner assign bills to the paycheck that covers them

For each paycheck below, assign the bills it needs to cover, check them off as you pay them, and see what's left over to spend. Click the ▾/▸ arrow next to a paycheck to collapse or expand its bill list — the assigned/leftover totals and the paycheck amount stay visible either way, so it's easy to skim past paychecks you've already sorted out. Paying more (or less) than the usual amount this time? Use Edit on that bill — for one not logged yet, use Log & edit amount to log it and adjust before it's due; either way, it only changes that one occurrence, not the recurring template's default. Paying one bill from two different paychecks? Use its Split button (here or in the list below) to divide it into two linked entries with the amount and paycheck you choose for each. Adding income and one-time expenses now happens on the Check Register tab.

Check register

Type a new transaction into the blank row at the bottom — just pick an account. Paying down a credit card? Pick Pay down: <card> instead of a category — it'll show up on the CC Register tab automatically, no need to enter it twice. Bank imports land here flagged with ✗ until reviewed. Every field is editable right on the row — date, description, amount, account, cleared, paid, and paycheck — nothing opens a separate panel; each change saves the moment you leave the field or press Enter. The italic Beginning balance row at the top isn't a transaction — click its date or amount to set it directly, and every balance below adjusts to match. A Paycheck field shows up on every expense's row — entirely optional, just a way to earmark a one-off expense (an extra card payment, say) against whichever paycheck it's meant to come out of, same as a bill from the Recurring tab. Typed a recurring bill straight into the register instead of waiting for it to auto-log? Just pick the same account (and card/type, if it's a card payment) the bill normally uses — it's linked to the bill automatically, and the reminder that was already logged for it quietly disappears. You'll see a Linked to <bill> tag on the row when that happens, with an Unlink button in case it ever guesses wrong. (Jumping in from Search, the Dashboard, or elsewhere still opens the full edit panel — that's just for finding your way here quickly.) Uncleared rows always sink to the bottom of whatever order the table's currently sorted in, so an unreconciled item never sits in the middle of the list throwing off the balance you're checking against — check a row off cleared and it drops right back into its normal place.

Your credit cards

Each card keeps its own running balance, separate from your spending categories. Log a transaction below with its real category (e.g. Shopping) and the card it went on — a charge adds to that card's balance, a payment brings it down. Already recording card payments in the Check Register? Pick Pay down: <card> there instead of a category and it'll show up here automatically. Leave As of blank when adding a card so every transaction you've already logged for it counts toward the balance — only set a date there if you specifically want the running balance to start fresh from that point on (anything dated earlier won't show a balance).

CC Register

Type a new charge or payment into the blank row at the bottom — pick the card it goes on, then the row underneath it to set the real spending category. A charge counts as spending in that category and increases the card's balance; a payment just pays the card down and isn't counted as spending. Every field on an existing row — Date, Description, Amount, and (underneath it) Category/Card/Type — is editable right in place, no separate panel; click Delete to remove it. A payment that matches a recurring card bill (same card and type) links to it automatically, absorbing the reminder already logged for it — you'll see a Linked to <bill> tag with an Unlink button in case it ever guesses wrong. Rows tinted light blue were entered via the Check Register, expense form, or a recurring Pay down item, not directly on this tab. A transaction dated after today shows upcoming instead of a balance — it isn't reflected in the card's current balance until that date arrives, matching the "Current balance" card above. Uncleared rows always sink to the bottom of whatever order the ledger's currently sorted in, so an unreconciled charge never sits in the middle of the list throwing off the balance you're checking against — check a row off cleared and it drops right back into its normal place.

Import from your bank

Most banks (including Bank of America) let you download recent transactions as a CSV from their website. Upload that file here to bulk-add them instead of typing each one in by hand. Nothing is sent anywhere — the file is only read in your browser.

Backup & export

Your data lives only in this browser. Export regularly to avoid losing it, or to move it to another device.

The CSV export includes a Register column so you can tell Check Register rows from CC Register rows once it's open in Excel -- or narrow it to just one below before exporting. A card payment counts as both (it's a real bank transaction that also shows on the CC Register), so it's included whenever either box is checked.

Reset

Permanently deletes all expenses, categories, and recurring items in this browser.
